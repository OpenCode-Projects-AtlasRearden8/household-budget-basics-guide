# The Weekly Ten-Minute Money Date

Ten minutes, once a week, is what keeps every other chapter on the road. No marathon spreadsheet sessions. No 47-tab Sunday of shame. A short, warm, boring-but-kind check-in with your own numbers — alone, or with your person over coffee.

The date's only goal: notice what's real, and let the plan absorb it. You are not *auditing yourself*. You're a pilot glancing at instruments.

## The script (use these five steps in order)

Set a phone timer for ten minutes and do, in order:

1. **Check the engine light (1 min).** Glance at checking balance and [Holding Account](04-irregular-paychecks.html) if you have one. Compare against what the [paper plan](01-paper-first-month.html) says you should see. If it looks roughly normal — *and it will, most weeks* — that's the whole step. Breathe out.

2. **Look ahead 7–10 days (3 min).** Open the [bills calendar](02-bills-calendar.html). What's due before your next money date? Confirm each is funded or make it funded (that's what the buffer is for). This one step prevents about 90% of household money stress by itself.

3. **Spend the last 7 days a little (2–4 min).** Skim last week's card/transfers. Note anything that was outside the plan — no judgment, just a tally mark by the category on the [worksheet](08-worksheets.html). This keeps the paper month's real numbers honest without daily tracking.

4. **Fund the funds (1–2 min).** Transfers for the week's [sinking funds](03-sinking-funds.html) go out (or get ticked as "already scheduled"). If you borrowed from a fund this week, log the loan and its refill plan.

5. **One small win (1 min).** Do exactly one tiny thing: move $5 to a fund, mark a bill auto-paid, adjust one category, or simply write next week's one focus on the worksheet. Then you're done. Small wins are the entire engine of this system.

When the timer dings, stop even if something's unfinished — note it and it'll be there next week. Ten minutes, honestly kept, beats sixty minutes you keep putting off.

## Weekly questions worth stealing

Rotate these — you don't need all of them every week:

- What's coming up that I can see coming? (→ calendar, [sinking funds](03-sinking-funds.html))
- What did I *learn* from last week, not *fail* at?
- Is anything on autopilot that shouldn't be? (→ once a year, the [sweep](06-yearly-sweep.html) does this in depth)
- What's the one small thing that would make next week smoother?
- Is the [splurge line](05-splurge-line.html) being enjoyed? If joy isn't happening, that's a plan bug too — fix the line, not the person.

## With a partner (or a roommate, or alone)

- **Same page, always.** One shared note or worksheet. Surprises about money come from hidden pages, not from different opinions.
- **Roles, not surveillance.** One cover-the-bills, one look-ahead is fine. Both people can see everything; both people get veto power on new money commitments over an agreed amount (pick one: $50, $100, your call).
- **Risk batching for the big conversations.** If a hard topic comes up (income drops, big purchase, debt), say "let's put that on the list for Sunday's date" instead of hashing it at 10:45pm. Save it for the scheduled, fed, rested version of you both.
- **Alone works great.** Same timer, same five steps. Being your own calm teammate is the underlying skill of every chapter in this guide.

## When you miss a date (you will)

Skipping isn't failure, it's a data point about life. Two moves:

1. **Never skip two in a row.** The first skip is life; the second is a message. If life is genuinely heavy (new baby, night shifts, sick kid), shrink the date to five minutes: look ahead + fund the funds. The short version keeps the habit alive until the season passes.
2. **Stack it on an existing anchor.** Attach the date to something that already happens weekly: the Sunday grocery run, the Sunday laundry jack, the end of your last shift before two days off. Habits survive by piggybacking, not by willpower.

## What the date is not

- It is not a budget autopsy. Nobody in history has ever felt better after line-by-line coffee forensics. We do a light weekly glance, and once a month a fuller refresh of the paper plan.
- It is not an obligation to feel bad. Every number you find was already true before you looked; the date just puts *you* back in the driver's seat where the plan lives.
- It is not homework forever. Once your funds and calendar hum, many weeks the date genuinely takes four minutes. The ten is a ceiling, not a floor. Consider that the goal.

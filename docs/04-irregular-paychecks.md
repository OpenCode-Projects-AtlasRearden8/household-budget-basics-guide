# Irregular Paycheck Smoothing (for shift workers & variable income)

If your paycheck is never the same twice — nursing shifts, per-diem work, overtime that comes and goes, tips, gig apps, seasonal overtime — then the standard advice ("add up your monthly income") is useless. You can't add up a moving target.

The fix isn't better income prediction. It's making your **plan** use a steady number while your real income waves around. The income picks a size; the budget doesn't have to.

## Step 1 — Find your Baseline Month

Look at the last three to six months of income (bank statements, paystubs, however you track it). Write them down, then pick your **Baseline**: not the best month, not the worst — a honest, boring middle number.

- Banker's choice: the median of your last six months.
- Extra-safe choice: the number you're pretty sure you'll clear even when shifts get cut.

That Baseline is the number your [paper-first plan](01-paper-first-month.html) uses. All of it gets a job: bills, groceries, [sinking funds](03-sinking-funds.html), splurge line, buffer. Everything the Baseline can cover, on paper, every single month.

Fill this in on the [Variable Income worksheet](08-worksheets.html) — it does the median math for you if you're filling it in the browser.

## Step 2 — Build a Holding Account (the smoothing bucket)

This is the actual mechanism, and it's simple:

- **One separate savings account**, nicknamed something like "Paycheck Air Traffic Control."
- Every paycheck — big, small, whatever — **lands here first.**
- On payday, you transfer your **Baseline** amount to checking. Checking always behaves like you're paid that same steady amount, no matter what actually fell out of the sky.

When pay is light, the Holding Account quietly makes up the gap. When pay is heavy, the Holding Account refills. Checking life stays boring and predictable — which is the entire point. Your *spending* plan is stable even though your *work* isn't.

**How much to seed the Holding Account?** One Baseline month is the goal; start with whatever's real (even $200 or one good week of set-aside). Build it with the surplus from Step 3 and heavy months. Nobody builds this in a weekend; it's fine if it takes a season.

## Step 3 — Route the surplus on purpose

Extra income — overtime shifts, a bonus, four-shift week — is a wonderful problem, and it deserves a standing assignment decided *once*, in advance, before the money arrives:

1. **First: refill the Holding Account** until it holds one full Baseline month.
2. **Second: feed the thin funds** — whichever [sinking fund](03-sinking-funds.html) is furthest behind.
3. **Third: the fun bucket** — extra pay funds real treats (bonus rule: if you worked the overtime, you get a slice just for you, no guilt, that's the [splurge line](05-splurge-line.html) in overtime form).

Write that priority order down once. Then, on a heavy payday, you don't make a decision; you just follow your own orders from a smarter, calmer you.

## Special notes for shift workers

- **Night-shift differentials and weekend rates:** include them in the Baseline only if they're genuinely steady. Otherwise treat them as surplus and let Step 3 route them.
- **Per-diem or cancelled-shift weeks:** the Holding Account covers light weeks without you renegotiating the whole plan. Your bill due dates don't care why the check was small, so make checking-landings not care either.
- **Third paycheck months** (biweekly pay): most months have two paychecks covering the plan. When a third appears, it's pure surplus — route it through Step 3. This is why the biweekly crowd can build a Holding Account faster than almost anyone.
- **Partner income is also variable?** Then the shared Holding Account smooths both. Two waves, one harbor.

## If the bottom shelf slides

This chapter is for wave-shaped income, not for real shortfalls — when Baseline can't cover essentials even in normal months, that's a math problem, not a smoothing problem, and no system can polish it. Two moves:

1. Trim the flexible plan to essentials (temporarily — a plan you can actually cover beats a pretty one you can't).
2. Work the if-you-cut-it-where-it-hurts-least list on the [money date](07-money-date.html), one item at a time, with zero self-blame.

Iffy circumstances — medical bills, job gaps, collections — are the *Credit & Debt Calm Guide*'s home turf. This chapter just makes sure your own plan isn't adding avoidable chaos on top.

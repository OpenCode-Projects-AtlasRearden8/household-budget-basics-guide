# The Yearly Insurance & Membership Sweep

Once a year — pick a calendar day (your birthday is traditional, or the month your car insurance renews) — do a one-hour sweep of everything in your household that **renews, recurs, or quietly bills you**. The goal: keep the ones earning their keep, cancel or trim the rest, and specifically re-shop the two big ones (insurance and phone) that most households haven't priced in years.

This chapter is about *when and what*: a checklist you run so nothing gets forgotten. The companion **Subscription Audit Handbook** owns the line-by-line technique for finding and canceling subscriptions — use both together.

## The once-a-year checklist

Work down this list in one sitting, with statements and the [Yearly Sweep worksheet](08-worksheets.html):

### 1. Auto insurance (the biggest win on the list)
- Get at least two quotes on your current coverage (same deductibles and limits!) — rates drift up over time even when you do nothing wrong. Loyalty is frequently the most expensive thing you own.
- Check you're not paying for coverages that no longer match the car's age.
- Ask about genuine discounts (safe driver programs, multi-policy, paying-in-full).

### 2. Health & life insurance
- Re-read your plan's copays and deductibles once. Not fun; useful.
- Employer plans get re-checked at open enrollment, not just annually — put that window on the [bills calendar](02-bills-calendar.html).
- Life insurance: match the *actual* need (who depends on you, for how long), not a salesperson's number.

### 3. Phone, internet, and TV-ish things
- Call your provider yearly and ask two questions: "What's the cheapest plan that fits what we actually use?" and "Are there current promos for existing customers?" Two questions, one call, real money.
- Do this the week before your contract/AP term renews so they know you can leave.

### 4. Subscriptions & memberships
- Pull every recurring charge from the last twelve months of statements — streaming, apps, gym, clubs, FFXIV or game subs, meal kits, cloud storage.
- For each one: "Did I use this last month?" Fall, winter, seasonal signups don't get a pass just because a subscription *felt* useful once.
- Detailed cancel-lists and retention-offer scripts live in the **Subscription Audit Handbook** — bring its checklist to this sweep.

### 5. Annual fees and renewals on the calendar
- Car registration, property tax, renewals of licenses/professional dues (yes, nurses: license renewals count — move [sinking fund](03-sinking-funds.html math, chapter 3).
- Any "annual plan" you bought to save money: check the renewal date is still a good deal.

### 6. Bank & card fine print
- Any monthly maintenance fees (these should be $0 — plenty of accounts are free at credit unions).
- Interest rates on anything carrying a balance: the *Credit & Debt Calm Guide* has the move-by-move for that.

## The rules that keep sweeping honest

1. **Same-coverage comparisons only.** A cheaper quote always means less coverage until you've checked it's the same. Side-by-side, or it isn't a comparison.
2. **Cancel anything you can re-join.** Streaming that's leaving the catalog? Join again for one month if a show comes back. Most things worth keeping are easy to return to.
3. **Don't cancel out of shame.** A gym used twice a year is a decision to make once, on numbers ("$420/yr ÷ 2 visits = $210/visit"), and then either keep honestly or cancel without a monologue. No self-trial period needed. Numbers, then choice.
4. **Keep the winners.** Sweeps aren't austerity. If a membership genuinely carries your week, it goes back on the list with a smile and a renewal date on the calendar.

## What the sweep usually finds

Across the chapters of this project's guides, the recurring findings are: one duplicate insurance coverage, one phone plan three tiers too big, two subscriptions nobody remembers starting, and one renewal you were able to re-shop for hundreds less. Your mileage varies; finding **anything** in the first year is normal. The habit — not the hunt — is the asset.

Put your sweep day on the [bills calendar](02-bills-calendar.html) page-two right now, before you forget. It takes a year to pay off, and it does.
